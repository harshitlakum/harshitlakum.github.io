Computational Physics

This section highlights my computational physics projects. They range from advanced ESPResSo simulations of magnetically capped Janus colloids to fundamental explorations in quantum mechanics, molecular dynamics, and variational methods. Each project blends physics-based modeling with numerical algorithms and visualization.

VIEW
01

Field-Controlled Assembly and Structural Transitions in Shifted-Dipole Trimer Clusters

Using Brownian dynamics simulations in ESPResSo and an orientational order parameter S, this project investigates ring-to-chain transitions in magnetically capped Janus colloids. The work bridges theoretical predictions and experimental results by analyzing thermal fluctuations, interaction potentials, and structural stability in trimer clusters.

VIEW
02

Variational Monte Carlo Analysis of the Helium Atom

Applied the Variational Monte Carlo method to simulate helium’s ground state energy. Optimized trial wave functions using Monte Carlo integration and visualized convergence behaviors with Python, NumPy, and Matplotlib.

VIEW
03

InteractiveMDTools

An interactive Python-based toolkit for visualizing and manipulating molecular dynamics simulations in real time. Integrated ASE, ipywidgets, and Matplotlib for research and educational applications in molecular physics.

VIEW
04

SCFEnergyComputations

Implemented Self-Consistent Field (SCF) energy computations in Jupyter notebooks using PySCF. Applied Koopman’s theorem to derive ionization potentials and electron affinities, with visualizations of orbital energies.

VIEW
05

HartreeSlaterAnalysis

A project exploring Hartree products and Slater determinants in quantum chemistry. Developed interactive Jupyter notebook guides and visualizations to explain electron interactions and antisymmetry principles.

VIEW
06

HydrogenAtomSimulations

Simulated radial wave functions and spherical harmonics of the hydrogen atom in Python. Developed interactive notebooks for visualizing quantum states and atomic structures.

VIEW
07

The Free Particle and the Particle in a Box

Educational Jupyter notebooks illustrating two fundamental quantum systems: the free particle and the particle in a box. Included interactive visualizations of wave functions and boundary effects using NumPy, Matplotlib, and ipywidgets.
